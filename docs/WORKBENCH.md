> **Public edition:** Census town points and reviewed reference catalogs are bundled. Optional data collections retain their publishers' original location qualifications. Private find locations and personal data are excluded from Git. See the [README](../README.md) for the data inventory.

# Investigate a place inside Clovis

Clovis opens on **Atlas**: search published evidence, read a source record and
save it to the fieldbook. A town selection is optional. For local map research,
choose **Rocks & fossils** or **Choose town**, then inspect a public town or
exposed object and save what you learned with its source and date.

## Choose a place

Select a state, click the town box and type a name. Choose a returned Census
place, then select **Explore this town** directly below the town box. A name plus state, such
as `Santa Rosa, CA`, also works. For a rural location, use a nearby town.
**Change town** in the header or notes panel opens the same editable selector.
The compact **Try an example** action randomly selects and explores one of
100 public-town research questions, two distinct towns per state. It avoids
the immediately previous example. No example or sample observations load on
startup. After you click, the question appears as a compact summary with its
instructions closed. Click the question to expand the instructions. Dismiss
the example to retain the current map and report, or change your town to close it. The
example resets land-use history to unknown and follows your online geology
choice. It preserves saved notes. The historical catalog loads locally;
the example does not assert a fossil or object exists.
No address, browser location or sign-in is needed.

The header has four destinations: **Explore**, **Inspect a find**, **Fieldbook**
and **Research**. Explore follows a simple path: choose a place, follow a clue,
keep a record. **Options & land-use context** reveals the optional history and
online setting. **Statewide background and sources** offers every state's
reviewed learning context. Result actions appear after you build current
evidence; changing inputs clears that current-result state. **Refresh town
evidence** reruns the lookup. An offline report still contains dated historical
records and the selected state's fossil story; online rock maps have a separate status.

## Choose an appearance

Use **Dark / Light** in the header. The choice is kept locally in this browser
and applies after reload; it is separate from the fieldbook. Map tiles,
historical sheets, photographs and the logo retain their original colours.
Printed notes remain light. Keyboard and touch controls work in both themes.

## Read local evidence

The central **Rocks & fossils** tab gives the map room to explore. Below it,
open **Read rock evidence · materials, ages & sources** to read and select
mapped rock units. This drawer starts closed. Select a unit card to read its
material, interval, description and original source. Multiple
map units can overlap; they are separate map sources, not independent finds or
layers under your yard. Reviewed formation guides show fossil learning examples
only for matched named formations, with their own source and limits.

**Words in this source** explains the material and age vocabulary beside a
selected unit. Open a word for its meaning, an observation question and sources.
Age cards place periods or epochs in relative older-to-younger order; the
labels date the mapped unit, not a loose stone or historical object. Original
qualifiers and untranslated wording remain visible.

Open **Rock words & geological time · works offline** to search 28 material
concepts and 28 age labels even when rock maps are off or unavailable. Try
`loess`, `sedimentary`, `Ordovician` or `Lutetian`. Missing vocabulary is a
review gap. See the [reference sources](GEOLOGY_LEARNING_SOURCES.md).

Inside the rock evidence drawer, open **Map overlay & sources** for
**Show regional geology overlay**, which is
optional and off initially. It requires a
current successful online town report. Adjust its opacity to compare it with
the background map. Colours distinguish mapped units; a colour or blank tile
does not identify a specimen or prove absence. The overlay pauses in other
tools. Read the selected unit's source detail and any matched formation guide,
then use its investigation action to carry the source and interval into
written notes.

## Read a historical map

1. Open **Old maps**. The bundled catalog contains 50,578 atlas records under
   9,758 town/state names across all 50 states and DC. Exact town-name matches
   load locally. When no town match exists, the reader labels statewide records.
   Change date order, turn pages or choose statewide coverage. **Search more maps
   online** retains the live Library of Congress search.
2. Select a record card. Check the title, date and visible map extent. Search
   relevance does not guarantee it covers your chosen place.
3. Select **Load map sheets online** for a digitized record, then choose a sheet.
   An available preview can be viewed directly. Zoom, drag, rotate 90
   degrees or choose **Fit map**. Read street names, landmarks and the legend.
4. Write what you can actually see. Avoid treating an uncertain alignment as a
   property match. **Use this map in an investigation** opens guided review with
   source metadata; review it and save the written record to the fieldbook.

The catalog offers 12 records per page and up to 60 online sheets per record. Some towns have
no digitized matches; API or image access can also fail. The app shows that
status. Use **Open your own map image** for a JPEG, PNG or WebP under 12 MB when
you already have a suitable map. Add its title, date and source yourself.
Source-record links retain rights information: digitization does not establish
reuse rights or permission to collect.

## Search the atlas

**Atlas** uses one search across local source collections and one record reader.
Type a place, culture, material, fossil or source identifier. Choose a broad
evidence type when useful; **Limit to a source** is optional. Turn pages,
read the source conventions, and **Save source to fieldbook**. **Search coverage**
shows matching counts by source and collections that need downloading or repair.

**Manage offline collections** adds substantial downloadable packs. Select an
installed pack's **Explore in atlas** to search that source in the same reader.
For UCL occupation phases, **Associated evidence** links to the published
animal observations, plant observations and dating samples. Bone measurements,
recovery and parent phase/site links remain available. Associations stay within
their original dataset. See [collection coverage](DATA_COLLECTIONS.md).

Open **Specialist searches → Museum objects** for collection-specific filters.
Choose **Met · dated object references** or **Smithsonian · anthropology**,
type a term and press Enter or leave the search field. The two collections
contain 563,454 distinct source object records. Turn pages and select a record
to read its actual catalog fields. Change the Met object group or recorded-date
range to compare glass, ceramics, metal tools, coins, buttons or stone implements.
Dates belong to the museum object, not a separate find that resembles it.

Smithsonian's source text tags support browsing; they are derived from its
titles and object types. Its explicit cultures, catalog numbers and labeled
measurements remain source facts. This snapshot has no explicit object dates
or CC0 media URLs. Fields that are absent are omitted from the reader.

For an eligible Met record, **Load reference photograph** requests a
public-domain image. **Save reference to fieldbook** preserves the catalog
facts and source without attaching a photograph or inheriting a town. Open
Fieldbook, select the reference card, and prepare a question or compare notes.

Choose **Fossil specimens** to search 573,709 Smithsonian specimens by recorded
taxonomy, geological ages or formations. **Group by taxon** reduces repeated
specimens to source name/classification groups. Choose a group to read a sample,
then **Search these specimens** for its individual records. The reader preserves
classification, original age and formation labels, specimen number and type
citations where recorded. Missing fields are omitted. Individually CC0 images
load only after **Load reference photograph**; a failed image leaves the facts
readable. Save the specimen's source without asserting it occurs at your town.
See [fossil catalog sources](FOSSIL_REFERENCE_SOURCES.md).

**Object guides** contains 62 short readings on glass, ceramics, metals, coins,
buttons, hardware and stone. Each factual paragraph has its sources beside it.
Read manufacturing clues and dates in their stated context, then examine the
listed features and record what you can actually see. **Read object guides**
from Inspect opens the relevant material category. Saved guides remain source
learning notes, separate from your own observations. See
[guide sources](OBJECT_MATERIAL_GUIDE_SOURCES.md).

**Newspaper history** reads publication timelines, languages, frequency and
indexed places from 46,871 title records. Select a town using the existing place
controls, then choose **Selected town**, or browse a state or all indexed
titles. The LOC snapshot is partial: 190 of 637 pages were retrieved. Town matches
and missing results describe that prefix only; an absent title is not evidence
that a newspaper never existed. No articles or OCR are bundled. Save a title
citation before using its LOC source to locate contemporary accounts. See
[newspaper sources](NEWSPAPER_SOURCES.md).

**Mapped geology** reads the USGS national map's unit descriptions and original
source-map units offline. Choose selected-town, selected-state or national
scope, search an age or material, then select a unit. Town scope uses the public
Census point at 1:500,000 scale. Expand a recorded source unit to read its full
description and citations. The four map themes are separate mapping products,
not a measured profile beneath a yard. Save the regional map context with its
source citations. The live Macrostrat reader and overlay remain available in
Rocks & fossils. See [USGS sources](USGS_UNIT_LIBRARY_SOURCES.md).

Choose **State fossil stories** to read three cited facts for any of 52 states
and territories, including named taxa, geological intervals and formation
stories. This chapter selector changes what you read; it does not change
the town on your map. See [Met sources](OBJECT_REFERENCE_SOURCES.md),
[Smithsonian sources](SMITHSONIAN_REFERENCE_SOURCES.md), and
[state fossil sources](STATE_FOSSIL_SOURCES.md).

## Turn evidence into an investigation

In **Library**, choose **Archaeological projects** to search published
excavations, surveys, analyses and databases from Open Context. It works
without selecting a town. Search a title, creator, subject or short description,
or filter by the source's recorded country context. Read a card for its
attribution, archaeological time coverage, publication date and reuse license.
Missing country and date fields stay explicit. **Save publication to fieldbook**
preserves the source without inheriting your town. Full project data opens at
its publisher. The displayed coverage count describes this publisher snapshot;
it is not a count of all digs. See [archaeology project sources](ARCHAEOLOGY_PROJECT_SOURCES.md).

In **Investigation**, choose one of three guided tasks:

| Task | Useful result |
| --- | --- |
| Read the rocks | Map source and interval compared with visible grains, layers or imported fill. |
| Trace a place through time | Dated map source and visible streets, buildings or land-use clues. |
| Document an exposed object | Material, features, scale and questions for an expert. |

Tick only the steps you completed. Add observations and select **I cannot tell
yet**, **Some observed details match**, or **Observed details differ**. These
are your comparisons, not verified identities. A valid town and written
observation are required to save. Source text is limited to 1,000 characters,
date/interval to 120 and observations to 1,500.

**Inspect a find** has more detailed material comparisons and a browser-local
photo pad. Document what is already exposed; the workflow is not a dig plan.
Its feature-specific question cards appear first in the center. Select
**Read the questions for these features** to reach them directly, then
**Record this detail** to focus your observations without changing them.
**Read the full comparison notes & sources** opens the longer guide when needed.
Save and download actions are above the cards. An optional local photo viewer
lets you zoom, drag, rotate and fit the image; **Remove photo** clears it from
the app. This does not change the original file. Photo instructions expand
when needed. Public town context is optional: check
**Include [town]** only when that town belongs with the observation. Changing
town clears the choice and preserves your written notes. The saved record
keeps the included town without exact coordinates or a claim about provenance.
Possible-bone guidance appears next to the material selector immediately.

## Return to your work

**Fieldbook** keeps town reports, guided investigations and object
observations together. Open a card, compare another record or add a dated
follow-up. The original remains intact. Print selected notes or export JSON to
back them up or move devices. Restore merges valid entries. Limits remain 50
entries and 750 KB. Older Clovis versions that do not know guided investigations
cannot read those new entries; use this version or newer to restore them.

For structured field observations choose **Fieldbook → Dig records**. Create a
named local project, record its units and contexts, then link finds and samples
to the recorded context. Depth requires its unit and named datum; bag numbers
remain separate. Source documents and above/below/same-as context relationships
retain explicit links. Corrections preserve dated revisions and stable IDs.
Download a project ZIP backup to move devices; matching histories merge and
conflicting corrections are refused. See [dig records](DIG_RECORDS.md) for
storage and bounds. This local notebook supplies no team accounts or archive
submission.

## Prepare a question and record a response

In **Fieldbook**, select the observation or investigation you want help with.
Open **Prepare a question about these notes**, write your question and optionally
add a possible comparison and a detail you want help with. **Preview question
packet** shows exactly what you can download or save. Editing the fields clears
that preview; preview again before saving. The packet includes the saved source
notes and only the broad town context already included in that record. Photos
are separate and are not attached. Clovis contacts nobody.

On a saved question, open **Record a response to this question**. Record a public
institution or role, the actual response date, what it said and what remains
uncertain. Saving creates a separate record and preserves the original notes
and question. Attribution is user-recorded, not verified by Clovis. Use
**Related saved records** to return to the source or question and add another
response. Readers, note downloads and printed text lead with the new question
or response, followed by preserved source notes; JSON backups retain the
original snapshots unchanged.

## Privacy and evidence limits

In **Library → Mineral properties**, search the 6,381-record Wikidata snapshot
for formulas, hardness, density and crystallographic properties. Read original
values with units, qualifiers and statement sources. Save a source reference
to Fieldbook; it inherits no selected town and does not identify your find.


- Town lookup is bundled. Online geology sends the public Census town point
  and normal network metadata to Macrostrat only when Explore is pressed.
- The geology overlay requests viewport tiles directly from Macrostrat;
  background maps also request tiles from their providers.
- Historical search sends the public town and state from the local app server
  to Library of Congress. Selected map images load from its image service in
  the browser. The app does not download an archive of those images.
- Local map images and find photos remain in the browser. They are not sent to
  Python, analyzed, saved in the fieldbook or included in exports. Local map
  mode sends only a mode flag and the public town code, not image bytes.
- Saved readings and their browser fieldbook are processed by the local server
  for callbacks and remain in browser storage, specific to this device and app
  origin. Structured **Dig records** are written to the local computer's
  `Clovis Local/dig-records.sqlite`, outside the package and repository. Their
  project backups are separate. Keep sensitive details out of notes you intend
  to share.

The app does not calculate backyard discovery odds, verify property history,
identify an object or authorize excavation. Coverage gaps stay visible.

Provider interfaces: [Library of Congress API](https://www.loc.gov/apis/json-and-yaml/requests/endpoints/)
and [Macrostrat data services](https://dev.macrostrat.org/docs/data-services).
See [resident context](RESIDENT_CONTEXT.md), [observations](OBSERVATIONS.md)
and [setup](SETUP.md) for broader workflow and data details.
