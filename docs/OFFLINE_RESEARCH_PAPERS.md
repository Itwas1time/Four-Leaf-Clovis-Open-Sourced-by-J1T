# Full research papers and original tables

In **Atlas**, open **Limit to a source** and choose **Full research papers &
original tables**. Search `Kakapel`, `Kenya`, `Senegal`, `Ravin Blanc X`, `Cambodia`, `Longvek`, `Panama`, `Cerro Juan Díaz`, or an original DOI such as
`10.1371/journal.pone.0335678.s009`. An exact DOI opens only that source component.
The paper and its supporting tables have separate records and citations.

**Read complete source offline** opens the full reading edition. Each paper has
a contents list, methods, references and its original main tables. Each supporting workbook has readable sheets with original cell addresses,
units, number formats and publisher highlights. Wide tables scroll sideways;
cell details expose the literal numeric token and source number format.
Long notes occupying a single source cell appear above the grid with their
original cell address, so a title or preservation note does not hide the data.
Formulas retain their original formulas and cached results and are not
recalculated. Blank cells remain blank.

**Download complete study for offline reading** produces one ZIP containing
the full reading edition, unchanged XLSX originals, the original XML,
readable text and source-edition metadata. Extract it and open `index.html` in
a browser on another device. All reading links and styles are local. The
Panama figure captions are retained without images. The Cambodia study includes
its publisher analytical figures 4-7; figures 1-3 are not bundled.

**Save source to fieldbook** retains the complete selected component, source ID,
edition, attribution and original values. Saved components
retain the exact native original: compressed XML for the paper and original
XLSX bytes for a selected table. The largest workbook rebuilds its readable projection from the retained XLSX;
other components retain their reading projections alongside the original. Save the paper
and the tables relevant to your question, then add a follow-up with the question,
filters, findings and uncertainties. Export the fieldbook to reopen those saved
readings. The complete study ZIP preserves every native workbook, including its
layout and metadata, separately from the fieldbook's selected readings.

## Panama edition and scope

Sharpe, Ashley E.; Smith-Guzmán, Nicole E.; Díaz Pérez, Claudia Patricia;
Sánchez Herrera, Luis Alberto; Carvajal-Contreras, Diana; Curtis, Jason;
Cybulski, Jonathan D.; Desjardins, Benoit; Isaza-Aizpurúa, Ilean;
Kamenov, George; Núñez-Cortés, Yajaira. (2025). *Isotopic evidence of increased
societal diversification in Pre-Columbian Panama*. PLOS One 20(11), e0335678.
[Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0335678).

The publisher's manuscript edition was acquired on 23 October 2026. Its original
XML SHA-256 is
`35aeda403c4b04a1e89c306d06ed3c0d2c63eafa736f7b88cc57323d549b49e5`.
The original publication and supporting tables carry the publisher's
[CC0 dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/).
The retained citation identifies all authors. No licence is inferred for works
merely cited in the bibliography or for separately unbundled figure images.

The study principally concerns Cerro Juan Díaz (LS-3), central Panama. It includes
archaeological context, sample selection, laboratory methods, isotope values,
preservation criteria and uncertainty. It is one publication and ten supporting
tables, adding zero excavation events or newly enumerated specimens. Individual
people, tooth samples, bone samples, analytical matrices and aggregate statistics
are different populations. The paper reports 49 individuals, while its main
Table 1 reports 42 for bone collagen/apatite and 48 individuals (84 teeth) for
enamel. These columns overlap and must not be added as unique people.

The S3/S4/S6 captions and yellow highlights identify previously reported data.
The S2 table includes already published comparison data and an explicitly
described adjustment to modern plant values. Preserve those qualifications
when using it. S7 coordinates locate modern river-water sampling, not
archaeological finds or properties. Missing records in this study do not
establish missing archaeology elsewhere in Panama.


## Cambodia edition and scope

Polkinghorne, Martin; Morton, Catherine Amy; Roberts, Amy; Popelka-Filcoff,
Rachel S.; Sato, Yuni; Vuthy, Voeun; Thammapreechakorn, Pariwat; Stopic, Attila;
Grave, Peter; Hein, Don; and Vitou, Leng. (2019). *Consumption and exchange in
Early Modern Cambodia: NAA of brown-glaze stoneware from Longvek, 15th-17th
centuries*. PLOS One 14(5), e0216895.
[Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0216895).

The publisher manuscript and four supporting workbooks were acquired on
28 October 2026. The manuscript XML SHA-256 is
`7a607c5c792ee7044a5b711d57d4826aa1efca3a67d538244ab3c4ec0bbd913b`. The
publisher applies the [CC0 dedication](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/)
and states that the relevant data are in the paper and supporting files. The
full article, its main table, sample list, raw neutron-activation data,
principal-component summary and group summary are available offline. Four
publisher analytical figures are included; figure captions for 1-3
remain, but those images are not bundled. Included figures travel in the
complete study ZIP rather than a saved fieldbook note.

The paper describes 102 sherd samples, including reference material from Thai
production centres as well as material from Longvek. The original sample IDs,
context references, source categories, units, numeric tokens and reported
uncertainties are retained. The raw data workbook notes report mg/kg, two
standard deviations for uncertainties and detection limits even when no
concentration was determined; uranium is reported for U-238 only. Separate
measurements and tables overlap and are not additional specimens or excavations.
Study geography does not establish a private-property location or identify an
object. Missing or undetermined measurements do not establish absence.

Large workbooks open one sheet at a time; exceptionally large sheets are divided
into row ranges, with column headings in the first range. A saved copy retains the exact
XLSX and reconstructs its complete readable cells locally when opened; formulas
are never recalculated. Use **Download complete workspace** to carry selected
readings and project records together, or download the study ZIP to carry every
source component and included figure.

## Senegal edition and scope

Pruvost and colleagues (2025), *A Later Stone Age quartz knapping workshop and
fireplace dated to the Early Holocene in Senegal: The Ravin Blanc X site (RBX)*,
PLOS One 20(9), e0329824. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0329824).
The source edition retains all thirteen authors and the publisher's
[CC BY 4.0 license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Its manuscript
XML SHA-256 is `714894a023362da1200b1c7e0196cdce227ac632eb89ff4897cde38ed3b5096d`.

The full article and its 14 main tables describe excavations at Ravin Blanc X;
the S1 workbook contains the published detailed inventory of lithic material
from layer 2 of RBX-1. Original inventory labels, categories, French remarks,
measurements and cells remain intact. Some rows describe fragments or refits;
rows are not an additional count of unique objects or excavations. The portable
study also includes the publisher's inclusivity questionnaire as its original
DOCX. Figure captions are retained, but the figure images are not bundled.
Published site locations do not identify private property or new finds, and an
absence from this study does not establish an absence of archaeology elsewhere.

## Kakapel Rockshelter edition and scope

Goldstein and colleagues (2025), *Cultural, economic, and settlement shifts over
the last 9,000 years at Kakapel Rockshelter, Western Kenya*, PLOS One 20(8),
e0328805. [Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0328805).
The source edition retains all seventeen authors and the publisher's
[CC BY 4.0 license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

The full article and its five main tables report excavation, a survey of 14 rock
shelters in the Chelelemuk Hills (Busia County), radiocarbon dates, ceramics,
chipped stone and raw materials. The S1 Table workbook holds the published
measurements and attributes of the shell-bead assemblage, with its trench,
context and identifier labels (including the authors' own question marks). The
portable study also carries S1 Text, the excavators' descriptions of the
archaeological contexts, as its original DOCX; its context numbers correspond
to the radiocarbon table. Figure captions are retained, but figure images and the
three supplementary TIFF figures are not bundled. Radiocarbon, phase and
assemblage tables overlap and are not independent finds or events, and bead rows
are an assemblage sample, not a count of excavations. Published shelter
coordinates do not identify private property or a newly observed object, and an
absence from this study does not establish an absence of archaeology elsewhere.
